description: When asked to add a rule, record the rule — never a guessed justification for why the user wanted it

  type: feedback
---

When the user asks for a rule — a blocklist entry, a convention, a preference — record **the rule**, not a rationale you invented for it. Writing a "why" reads as though their reasoning were established, and it is usually wrong: you don't know why they asked.

**Why:** asked to add "smoking gun" to the overused-phrases list, I wrote a paragraph explaining that detective framing "inflates ordinary evidence into a reveal" — my own theory, presented as theirs. The correction was blunt: *"you don't have to add detailed explanations why not to use them (they are especially weird because you have no idea why i asked you to, and you come up with your own guesses)."* The section's own instruction already said to add the phrase **with its replacement**, not with a justification — so the invented rationale was both unasked-for and against the written format.

**How to apply:** for a rule, preference, or blocklist entry, write the entry, its scope, and what to do instead. Stop there. If the *why* genuinely matters for judging future edge cases and the user didn't give one, ask — don't supply one. This does not apply to findings you researched yourself, where the evidence IS yours to state: a measured cost, a traced failure path, a quoted doc. The line is whether the reasoning is something you established or something you attributed.
