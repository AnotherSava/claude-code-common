When the ask is "which of these", the artifacts are the evidence. Show the passage that decides each one alongside the question, rather than a sentence summarising your own conclusion about it.

2026-09-15: having built a `platform:` tag for the memo backlog, I asked "tag either as `windows`?" and gave one sentence per memo characterising what bound it. The reply was "show me memos in question". Both bodies were already in my context — printing them cost nothing, and the decision came back in the next message.

**Why:** a one-line summary is enough to *report* a judgement and not enough to *delegate* one. The reader is being asked to weigh something they cannot see, so the round trip spent retrieving it was built into the way the question was posed. The same words that make a good report make a bad ask.

**How to apply:** for a choice between specific artifacts — files, memos, candidate designs, rows of data — quote the decisive passage of each with the question. Where the artifact is long, quote the part the decision turns on and say the rest is available, rather than paraphrasing the whole.
